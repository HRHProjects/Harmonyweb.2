CraftyWand Brings Advanced Packaging Design Within Reach of Smaller Brands

The browser-based workspace combines parametric dielines, live folding previews and more than 6,400 mockups, with released features available free to registered users.

Published October 10, 2026 · Source: NorthwardAB

A Canadian-developed platform is making a direct challenge to the cost of digital packaging design. CraftyWand combines parametric dieline generation, live folding previews and more than 6,400 mockups in a browser-based workspace, with all currently released features available for free to users who create an account.

For small businesses, designers and independent creators, the proposition is straightforward: explore a product idea, develop its packaging and visualize the result without first taking on a software subscription.

A connected packaging design workflow

The platform brings together work that often spans several stages of a product launch. Users can adjust supported packaging structures to their dimensions, generate dielines and watch a flat layout fold into a three-dimensional package. They can apply artwork, inspect how it sits across panels and view the result from different angles.

That connection between structure and appearance helps make packaging easier to evaluate. A design that works on a flat page may look different when assembled. Live folding gives users a way to examine those relationships before taking a concept further with a designer or manufacturer.

More than 6,400 product mockups

CraftyWand’s mockup library extends the workflow beyond boxes and cartons. It includes bottles, pouches, cups, T-shirts, hats and other branded products, allowing users to explore how an identity works across both merchandise and packaging. A YouTube creator developing a merchandise collection can consider apparel and drinkware alongside the packaging around them. A small retailer can compare product concepts, while a freelance designer can present a broader picture of a client’s brand.

A different pricing model

The commercial difference becomes clearer when compared with established alternatives. Pacdora’s free plan restricts capabilities such as dieline-template exports and watermark removal, while paid subscriptions unlock additional functionality. Packify offers a one-time allowance of free credits, followed by paid plans for continuing access to its credit-based services. CraftyWand’s current offer removes the subscription charge for access to its released tools.

Those competing brands are also closely connected. Packify describes itself as Pacdora’s AI-native packaging brand, and both Packify and Pacdora share a virtual office space in Singapore. Their China-based parent company, Baoxiaohe, lists Pacdora as its international edition and identifies a Hangzhou based company as its developer.

A Canadian-developed alternative

CraftyWand therefore introduces to the world an accessible data safe Canadian-developed alternative to this connected product family. For businesses considering the companies behind their design tools, that expands their choice alongside the immediate differences in features and cost.

Since its launch in July, CraftyWand says its website has received more than 5,000 new users each month. The team is continuing to improve the platform and add features as it seeks to turn that early interest into a lasting place in users’ workflows.

“In the coming months, we plan to launch AI features including a specialized packaging agent designed to manage the concept-to-shelf journey, while developing print partnerships to bring design and production into one connected workflow that saves users time and money,” says CraftyWand founder Sharmake Korane.

The ambition is global. By combining structural design, visualization and an extensive mockup library without a subscription barrier to its released features, CraftyWand is building a clear commercial proposition: advanced packaging tools should be accessible to the next independent brand as readily as they are to an established business.
